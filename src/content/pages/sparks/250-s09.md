# S09 · A form that asks

## The problem

You're collecting RSVPs for an event. The first draft of the form looks fine with a mouse and fails almost everyone else: each box's only label is grey placeholder text that **vanishes as soon as you type**, a row of radio buttons has **no question**, so a screen reader hears "Saturday, radio button, 1 of 3" and nothing else, every required field is **red before anyone has typed**, and when something is wrong, the **only sign is the colour**.

Your job is the "Wanted" side: a new RSVP page with a form anyone can fill in, that lands them on a thank-you page. Plan on 15–30 minutes. It's due before class on Mon Nov 23 and graded Complete / Not yet straight from your repo, so there's nothing to submit.

It's a new page, not a copy of an earlier Spark, and it's half of P4 (due Tue Nov 24), which asks for a different form. Use the unit's [At a glance](/250/units/forms/#at-a-glance) table and Live demo as your reference. The step most people miss is the error style: `:invalid` turns every required field red before anyone types, so use `:user-invalid`, and say what's wrong in words, not only in red.

**Your Final Project's first milestone is due Mon Nov 23 too:** your intent brief, in `final-project/INTENT.md`. It's ungraded, and it's on the [Final Project](/250/final-project/#milestones) brief's milestone map. If you started last week's version of this Spark (the intent brief), move your words into `final-project/INTENT.md`; they count there.

## 1. Intent

Create the new folder `sparks/s09-forms/` with its own `NOTES.md` (copy `starter/NOTES.md`, or ask the agent to). Then write two or three sentences in its **Intent** section: who RSVPs, on what device, and what they most need to know before they send it. Pick an event:

- a book club meeting: name, email, which book night, and anything they'll bring,
- a group hike: name, email, which trail, and whether they need a ride,
- a volunteer shift: name, email, which shift, and which jobs they'll do,
- a study group: name, email, which time, and which topics they want to cover,
- a **Saturday Morning Archive** watch party: name, email, which morning, and which shows they'd vote for,
- or your own idea, for a real or realistic audience.

**Predict before you prompt.** Under your intent, add a line that starts `Prediction:`. Look at the sketch and the unit's At a glance table, and name the elements, attributes, and selectors (not their values) you expect to fix each part of the problem: the vanishing labels, the radio buttons with no question, the red-from-the-start fields, and the colour-only error. A guess is fine; you'll check it in step 5.

## 2. Spec: the "done" list

Your form is done when:

<!-- done:start -->

- [ ] `sparks/s09-forms/` has its own `index.html`, `thanks.html`, and CSS file, and it's a new page, not a copy of an earlier Spark.
- [ ] Both pages pass the [W3C validator](https://validator.w3.org/nu/).
- [ ] The form has `action="thanks.html"` and `method="get"`, and `thanks.html` has a heading, a sentence saying what happens next, and a link back.
- [ ] Every field has a visible `<label>` joined by `for` and `id`, and no `placeholder` stands in for a label.
- [ ] The name and email fields have `required`, the right `type`, and an `autocomplete` value.
- [ ] One set of radio buttons (one choice) and one set of checkboxes (any of these) each sit in a `<fieldset>` whose `<legend>` asks the question.
- [ ] One field has a hint tied to it with `aria-describedby`, and its words change (or gain a "Check this:") when the field is wrong.
- [ ] Error styles use `:user-invalid`, not `:invalid`: nothing is red until someone has changed a field or tried to send.
- [ ] You can fill in and send the whole form with the keyboard alone, with visible focus.
- [ ] `NOTES.md`'s Intent has your `Prediction:` line, and under it a `What it used:` line comparing your guess with the code.
- [ ] Your hub `index.html` links to it under Sparks.
- [ ] `NOTES.md` covers every section, in your own words.
- [ ] It's committed with a meaningful message, pushed, and the live page loads.

<!-- done:end -->

**Optional extra (not graded):** add a `<select>` (which question would you use one for instead of radio buttons, and why?), or an optional field with a `type` the browser checks, such as `url`. Say in `NOTES.md` what the browser says when you get it wrong.

## 3. Generate

Give the agent your intent and the "done" list (not your prediction: that's for you), and ask it to propose a plan before it writes any HTML: each field, its label, its `type`, and which fields are grouped under which question. Then have it build the two pages.

```text
Here's my intent and my "done" list for sparks/s09-forms/. Before you write any HTML, list each field you'd use: its label text, its type and autocomplete value, whether it's required, and which fields share a fieldset and what its legend asks. Wait for me to agree.
```

**Watch for:** a `placeholder` doing a label's job, `:invalid` instead of `:user-invalid`, radio buttons with different `name`s (then several can be chosen), a `<fieldset>` with no `<legend>`, an error shown only by colour, and JavaScript added to check the form, which `AGENTS.md` rules out and HTML already does. If you catch one, it's a great entry for "Accepted / changed / rejected."

## 4. Verify

Walk the sketch on your own page:

- Type in each box. Does its label stay on screen? Click each label: does the cursor jump into its field?
- Reload. Is anything red? Press Send with everything empty: does the browser stop and say what's wrong, in words, and only now turn the fields red?
- Put `abc` in the email box and send. Does your hint change its words?
- Put the mouse away. Tab through the whole form, choose with the arrow keys and Space, and send it. Can you see where you are the whole time?
- On the thank-you page, read the address bar: your made-up answers are in the URL after the `?`. Then run the validator on both live pages.

## 5. Explain

**Check your prediction.** Under your `Prediction:` line, add `What it used:`: the elements, attributes, and selectors the agent actually used for each part of the problem, where your guess matched, where it didn't, and why. A wrong guess you can now explain is worth more than a right one you can't.

Then finish `NOTES.md`: what you kept, changed, or rejected and why, one thing you looked up on MDN ([`/dig-deeper`](/250/skills/dig-deeper/) on your `:user-invalid` rule or a `<legend>` is a good start), and anything you still don't understand.

Then run [`/check`](/250/skills/check/) `sparks/s09-forms` and paste in the "done" list above (the **Copy** button copies it).
