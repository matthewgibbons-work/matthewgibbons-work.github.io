# S09 · Final Project intent brief

Write a one-page intent brief for your [Final Project](/250/final-project/), yourself: who your Digital Humanities site is for, a first draft of your definition, the three real DH projects you'll use as examples, and the "done" list you'll check it against on Dec 12. The agent only turns your words into a valid HTML page. Plan on 15–30 minutes. It's due before class on Mon Nov 23 and graded Complete / Not yet straight from your repo, so there's nothing to submit.

It's the most important page you'll write this term, and the agent can't write it for you, any more than it can write your `NOTES.md`: say no if it offers to improve it, suggest a reader, define DH for you, or write your "done" list. You can ask it to help you *find* DH projects to look at, but you choose them, and you open each one yourself. Keep the plan to HTML and CSS; anything that needs JavaScript (search, logins, saving things) is out. The step most people miss is a "done" list someone else could check: "is accessible" can't be checked; "every image has `alt` text that fits its purpose" can.

## 1. Intent

Create `sparks/s09-final-project-intent/` with a `NOTES.md` copied from `starter/NOTES.md`.

Write your answers in plain words first, anywhere (a text file, a notebook). Use these headings:

1. **Who it's for.** One real or realistic person, and the question they'd ask you. "My roommate, a history major, who asked what I'd even do with a DH minor" beats "people curious about DH."
2. **My definition, first draft.** Two to four sentences: what the Digital Humanities are, in your words, today. It's allowed to change by Dec 12; your reflections page will say how.
3. **Three examples.** Three real DH projects, each with its link and one sentence on why it fits your definition and would matter to your reader. Open each link yourself.
4. **"Done."** Eight to twelve lines you'll check the finished site against, each one checkable with a yes or a no, and at least three about accessibility.
5. **Units.** At least four from this term you'll use, and where.
6. **One risk.** What's most likely to go wrong, and what you'll do if it does.

The pages are set by the brief: home, one page per example, and reflections. If an example looks too thin to fill a page, I'll tell you by Mon Nov 23.

## 3. Generate

Here the agent does only the markup. Paste your plain-text brief into the chat and ask:

```text
Turn this into a valid, accessible HTML page at sparks/s09-final-project-intent/index.html with the same headings. Don't change my wording. If anything is unclear, ask me instead of rewriting it.
```

If it offers to "improve" your brief, rewrite your definition, suggest a reader, or write your "done" list, say no. A better question to ask it: "Which of my 'done' lines would be hard to check, and why?" Then you fix them.

**Watch for:** your words quietly rewritten, "done" lines that can't be checked ("looks good", "is accessible", "works well"), and features that need JavaScript (search, logins, saving things), which `AGENTS.md` rules out.

## 4. Verify

- Read each "done" line aloud. Could someone else check it on your live site and answer yes or no? If not, rewrite it ("is accessible" becomes "every image has `alt` text that fits its purpose").
- Swap with a neighbour: can they say who your site is for, what your definition claims, and what "done" means, from your page alone?
- Open each example's link. Does it go to the project itself, and is it still online?
- Run the validator on the live URL.

## 5. Explain

Finish `NOTES.md`: why you chose these three examples over others you looked at, what (if anything) you kept or rejected from the agent, one thing you looked up on MDN, and what you're least sure about. That last one is a good first **Open question**.

Then run [`/check`](/250/skills/check/) `sparks/s09-final-project-intent` and paste in the "done" list above (the **Copy** button copies it).
