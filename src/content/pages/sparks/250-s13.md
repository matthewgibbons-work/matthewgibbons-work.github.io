# S13 · Your own minimal expression

## The problem

You want to define two pop-culture things you love the way today's build did: four colors and a font each, and a sentence on why. The first draft looks great on a laptop. On a phone, the two cards are **fixed at 500px wide, so the page scrolls sideways**; the color swatches are **squeezed into a row too narrow to read**; and one heading is written **in a bright accent color that fails contrast** on its own card.

Your job is the "Wanted" version, with your own two items: a new page whose cards fit any screen, lay themselves out by their own width, and keep every word readable. Plan on 15–30 minutes. It's due Wed Dec 9 (the last day for new-system work other than the Final Project), and graded Complete / Not yet straight from your repo, so there's nothing to submit.

The tools are the ones from today's build. Two things in the "done" list weren't in it: a heading in one of your accent colors (only if it passes as large text), and a container query breakpoint you choose yourself and can explain.

## 1. Intent

Create the new folder `sparks/s13-minimal-expression/` with its own `NOTES.md` (copy `starter/NOTES.md`, or ask the agent to). Pick **two** pop-culture items you know well: a show, a game, a band, a film, a book series. They don't have to be from Saturday mornings. In **Intent**, write two or three sentences: who would open this page, on what screen, and what you want them to recognise in each item's four colors and font.

**Predict before you prompt.** Under your intent, add a line that starts `Prediction:`. For each of your four colors, guess whether it could carry text on its card at 4.5:1, and which one you'd try for a large heading (3:1). A guess is fine; you'll check it in step 4.

## 2. Spec: the "done" list

Your Spark is done when:

<!-- done:start -->

- [ ] `sparks/s13-minimal-expression/` has its own `index.html` and CSS file, and it's a new page, not a copy of today's build or an earlier Spark.
- [ ] The page passes the [W3C validator](https://validator.w3.org/nu/), with landmarks and one `<h1>`.
- [ ] Your two items are cards in a list, in a grid made with `repeat(auto-fit, minmax(…))`, and nothing scrolls sideways at 320px or overlaps at 200% zoom.
- [ ] Each card is a container (`container-type: inline-size`), and a container query changes its layout at a width you chose. `NOTES.md` says why that width.
- [ ] Each card's four colors and its font are custom properties set on that card, and everything inside it uses the variables.
- [ ] Each card names its four colors in words (not color alone), with a sentence on why they and the font define the item.
- [ ] Body text on each card meets 4.5:1, and `NOTES.md` lists each pair you checked in WebAIM with its ratio.
- [ ] At least one heading uses one of your accent colors, and it meets 3:1 as large text (or `NOTES.md` says why none of yours could).
- [ ] No images and no tables.
- [ ] `NOTES.md`'s Intent has your `Prediction:` line, and under it a `What it used:` line comparing your guess with the ratios.
- [ ] Your hub `index.html` links to it under Sparks.
- [ ] `NOTES.md` covers every section, in your own words.
- [ ] It's committed with a meaningful message, pushed, and the live page loads.

<!-- done:end -->

**Optional extra (not graded):** add a third item, then narrow the window and watch the grid go from three columns to two to one. At which widths does it change, and where do those numbers come from?

## 3. Generate

Give the agent your intent, your two items with their colors and fonts, and the "done" list (not your prediction: that's for you). Ask for a plan before any code.

```text
Here's my intent, my two items with their four colors and fonts, and my "done" list for sparks/s13-minimal-expression/. Before you write any code, tell me: which two colors on each card you'd use for body text and the contrast ratio of that pair, which accent (if any) could be a large heading at 3:1, and the container query breakpoint you'd use for the cards and why. Wait for me to agree.
```

**Watch for:** fixed `width`s on cards instead of the grid deciding; `@media` where the brief asks for `@container` (or the container query set on the card itself instead of its contents); colors written straight into rules instead of through the variables; an accent heading that fails 3:1; swatches with no names; and a web font with no fallback. If you catch one, it's a great entry for "Accepted / changed / rejected."

## 4. Verify

Walk the sketch on your own page:

- Device toolbar at 320px: no sideways scroll. Then 200% zoom.
- Narrow the window slowly. When does a card change its layout? Is it the width you chose?
- Check each text pair in [WebAIM](https://webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker/), and your accent heading at 3:1. Write the ratios in `NOTES.md`.
- Cover the swatches with your hand: can you still tell what the colors are from the words?
- Run the validator on the live page.

## 5. Explain

**Check your prediction.** Under your `Prediction:` line, add `What it used:`: which colors could really carry text, which accent passed as a heading, and where your guess was wrong. A color you were sure about that failed is the best thing to write down.

Then finish `NOTES.md`: what you kept, changed, or rejected and why, one thing you looked up on MDN ([`/dig-deeper`](/250/skills/dig-deeper/) on your `@container` rule or `minmax()` is a good start), and anything you still don't understand.

Then run [`/check`](/250/skills/check/) `sparks/s13-minimal-expression` and paste in the "done" list above (the **Copy** button copies it).
