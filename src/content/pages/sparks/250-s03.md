# S03 · Color with contrast

**Concept:** color, with contrast you've checked and meaning that never depends on color alone.
**Time:** 15–30 minutes in studio, and due before class.

You'll recolor the Episode Guide you rebuilt in S02. The markup is familiar, so all your attention can go on color. The agent will happily pick colors. Your job is to prove they work, with numbers.

## 1. Intent

Copy your `sparks/s02-episode-guide-rerun/` folder to `sparks/s03-color-contrast/` (leave S02 as it is, since it's graded separately). Then write two or three sentences in the new folder's `NOTES.md` **Intent** section: what mood the colors should set, and for whom. "Bright and Saturday-morning, but readable on a phone in the sun" gives the agent something to aim at.

## 2. Spec: the "done" list

Your page is done when:

<!-- done:start -->

- [ ] `sparks/s03-color-contrast/` has its own `index.html` and CSS file, and S02 is unchanged.
- [ ] The page uses a palette of 3–4 colors, written once at the top of the CSS file with a comment naming each one.
- [ ] Every text color passes the [WebAIM contrast checker](https://webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker/) against its background: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text. Each pair and its ratio is listed in `NOTES.md`.
- [ ] At least one background is a `linear-gradient()`, and the text on it passes at the gradient's worst point.
- [ ] Links are underlined, and focus outlines are still visible on every background.
- [ ] One episode state (for example, "watched" or "favourite") is shown with a word or a labeled icon, not just a color.
- [ ] The page passes the [W3C validator](https://validator.w3.org/nu/) with no errors.
- [ ] Your hub `index.html` links to it under Sparks.
- [ ] `NOTES.md` covers every section, in your own words.
- [ ] It's committed with a meaningful message, pushed, and the live page loads.

## 3. Generate

Give the agent your intent and the "done" list, and ask it to propose a palette before it writes any CSS. Ask it for each pair's contrast ratio, then check every one yourself: the agent's numbers are a claim, not a result.

**Watch for:** ratios the agent states but didn't calculate, links with `text-decoration: none`, focus outlines that disappear against a new background, and states shown only by a color change. If you catch one, it's a great entry for "Accepted / changed / rejected."

## 4. Verify

- Check every text and background pair in the WebAIM checker, including the lightest point of your gradient. Write down the ratios.
- In Chrome DevTools, open **Rendering** (from the ⋮ menu → More tools) and set **Emulate vision deficiencies** to *Achromatopsia* (no color). Can you still tell which episodes are watched, and where the links are?
- Tab through the page. Can you see focus on every background?
- Run the W3C validator on the live URL.

## 5. Explain

Finish `NOTES.md`: what you kept, changed, or rejected and why, one thing you looked up on MDN ([`/dig-deeper`](/250/skills/dig-deeper/) on `hsl()` or `linear-gradient()` is a good start), and anything you still don't understand.

Then run [`/check`](/250/skills/check/) `sparks/s03-color-contrast` and paste in the "done" list above (the **Copy** button copies it).
