# Keep Copilot for the course

Copilot Free gives your GitHub account **one monthly allowance**. Everything you ask Copilot counts against it: in VS Code, on github.com, anywhere you're signed in with that account. If you spend it on other classes or personal questions, it can run out partway through a Spark.

**The rule:** work in your course repo goes to Copilot. Everything else goes to a second, free tool. Setup takes about 10 minutes.

## Part A of C · Pick a second tool (about 5 min)

Pick one. They're all free, and all fine for questions, writing help, and other classes.

| Tool | Sign up with | What free gets you | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com) | Any email, or a Google, Microsoft, or Apple account | Plenty of everyday text chat. File uploads and images are limited. | May show ads on the free plan. |
| [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com) | A **personal** Google account | Free Gemini. US college students can also get [a year of Google AI Pro](https://gemini.google/students) free. | The student offer needs a card and **renews at $19.99/month** unless you cancel. Set a reminder. It doesn't work with a school Google account. |
| [Microsoft Copilot](https://copilot.microsoft.com) | A personal Microsoft account | Free chat. Microsoft doesn't publish a fixed limit. | This isn't GitHub Copilot. It's a separate product and a separate allowance. |

None of these touch your GitHub Copilot allowance.

## Part B of C · Keep it separate (about 3 min)

This step keeps you from drifting back to Copilot out of habit.

- [ ] In your browser, make a new profile named **Personal AI**. Chrome: click your profile picture → **Add**. Edge: profile picture → **Add profile**. Safari: **Safari → Settings → Profiles**. Firefox: open `about:profiles` → **Create a New Profile**.
- [ ] Sign in to your second tool in that profile only.
- [ ] Pin it so it's one click away (bookmark bar, or install it as an app if the browser offers to).
- [ ] Optional: in the Personal AI profile, stay signed out of github.com, so you can't ask Copilot there by accident.

## Part C of C · What stays in Copilot

**Anything in your course repo.** Don't paste a Spark, Prototype, or Final Project into the second tool. Copilot reads your repo's `AGENTS.md` and has the course skills (`/check`, `/dig-deeper`). The other tools don't, so they'll happily use code beyond what you've covered, and you still have to explain every line you submit.

The second tool is fine for questions *about* the course, like "what's the difference between a class and an id?". But when you're building, use Copilot.

## Check your usage

- **In VS Code:** click the Copilot icon in the Status Bar (bottom of the window). It shows how much of this month's allowance you've used.
- **On GitHub:** [github.com/settings/copilot](https://github.com/settings/copilot) shows your plan and usage.

Check it once a week. If it's high early in the month, ask yourself what used it.

## Still running out?

- **Start a new chat for each task.** A long chat costs more with every message, because Copilot rereads the whole conversation each time.
- **Ask for one step at a time.** "Add the nav, then stop" costs less than "build the whole page" followed by three rounds of fixes.
- **Tell me before the deadline, not after.** If you've hit the limit with work still due, message me and we'll sort it out.

## A note on privacy

Whatever tool you use, don't paste in your own private information or anyone else's: passwords, ID numbers, grades, or other people's messages. Free tools may use your chats to train their models. Most have a setting to turn that off; look for "data controls" or "improve the model" in settings.
